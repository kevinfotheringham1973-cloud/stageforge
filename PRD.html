01Vision

StageForge is a cloud platform that gives capital and Hard FM programmes a single system of record for stage-gate delivery: every stage carries a deliverables checklist, every deliverable needs uploaded evidence, and a gate cannot close until a project sponsor signs off against that evidence.

The initial target is Hospital Hard FM. The same engine is meant to re-skin for other regulated Hard FM sectors — prisons, schools, defence, transport — under sector-specific names, e.g. "StageForth – Defence."

Assumption "Re-skin" is read here as one shared backend with sector-specific templates and branding, not separate codebases per sector — later confirmed by the multi-tenancy decision (see Sector / Brand Variants & Tenancy, §06).

The note also raises hosting StageForge as a sub-domain of pmopassport.co.uk.

Decided Confirmed: pmopassport.co.uk is a personally-owned domain, currently used by the GasNet project. StageForge would sit as a sibling subdomain under the same domain, not a shared company or product with GasNet.
Open question Does StageForge share any actual infrastructure or login with GasNet under that domain, or does it just coexist as an unrelated subdomain? Minor, low-stakes detail — doesn't block anything else in this doc.
Decided — evolution, confirmed 12 Sep 2026 The above described StageForge's original shape: a cloud platform where every dutyholder role (SRO, Fire Officer, Compliance Officer, Authorised Persons, Principal Designer, etc.) held a real login and interacted with the platform directly. Building that out to genuine completeness — every hospital asset system, every deliverable, the full compliance personnel model — surfaced a real adoption problem: an advanced project-management tool asking many people, several of them technophobes, to personally sign in and navigate it. A standalone Electron desktop build (Windows Store-certified, STAGEFORGE_LOCAL_MODE=1, its own local database per install) now exists specifically to solve this for the Project Manager: one person, in full control of the whole compliance landscape, using StageForge as their own cockpit — genuinely working, in active pilot use by real PMs. But Electron's local-mode convenience grants every role to whoever is running it locally, which is right for a PM's own navigation but is not genuine accountability — a PM could technically self-approve as SRO or Fire Officer on their own machine. Closing that gap without reintroducing the login burden Electron was built to remove is the point of the External Roster & Email-Based Approval work (§06) and its planned generalisation beyond Sponsor-only (§09 Phase 5): every real dutyholder decision reaches the right real person by email — with full context and the actual document attached, never a bare link — and their reply is the recorded decision, no login required, ever. This is the direction now — the "cloud platform, every role logs in" framing above describes where the product started, not where it's going.

02Problem it solves

Not stated directly in the source note, so this is inferred rather than confirmed: stage-gate sign-off on Hard FM programmes today likely runs on spreadsheets, email evidence trails, and spend tracked separately from delivery status. Without one record tying deliverable evidence, compliance status, resourcing, and actual spend to a single project number, a sponsor signs a gate off without a reliable single view of whether the project is actually ready. StageForge's job is to make "ready to sign off" a verifiable state rather than a judgement call.

Context Part of why this matters concretely for Health specifically: an NHS Trust doesn't always commission a Hard FM programme on its own discretionary timeline — sometimes it's driven by the Trust's own compliance obligations, or by a non-compliance finding that's been issued with a mandated closure date. In that setting, weak stage-gate governance isn't just messy paperwork, it's the difference between hitting or missing a deadline the Trust doesn't control. This stays narrative context for now, not a modelled feature (e.g. no Project.compliance_deadline field) — but it's the kind of scenario the platform should hold up under.

03Competitive landscape

Five named comparators, spanning two different categories:

  • Stage-Gate Navigator and Sopheon Accolade — the origin of the Stage-Gate methodology itself. Enterprise-grade, deeply configurable, built for new-product-development portfolios. Heavy to implement and priced for large innovation functions, not a single Hard FM contractor.
  • GenSight — enterprise portfolio management for NPD and IT PPM, custom-configured per client, cloud or on-premise. Same weight class as Sopheon: strong governance, long implementation, generic across industries.
  • Cerri Project — the closest existing shape to StageForge: configurable Stage-Gate criteria, Go/Kill/Hold/Recycle decisions, resourcing, and financial tracking in one platform. Still industry-agnostic, not built around a sector-specific compliance layer.
  • Smartsheet and Monday.com — generic work-management tools. Teams bend them into an approximation of a stage-gate checklist with automations, but neither has a native concept of a gate that structurally cannot close without evidence and a sponsor's sign-off, and neither ships a compliance layer.
Positioning None of the five are built for a specific regulated Hard FM sector with compliance treated as a first-class, concurrently-gating module alongside delivery. The Sopheon/GenSight/Cerri tier is powerful but generic-industry and enterprise-heavy to adopt; Smartsheet/Monday.com are flexible but have no structural gate enforcement at all. StageForge's opening is a purpose-built tool for Hard FM maintenance in hospitals first, where "gate closes only with evidence + sponsor sign-off + sector compliance satisfied" is the product, not a workaround built on top of a generic board.

04Who uses it

Decided Confirmed: the initial target user and buyer is Hard FM maintenance in hospitals. Four roles apply, and — importantly — none of them are fixed globally: who holds each role is set per project/programme in configuration, not hardcoded to a person or company.

05Core concepts

  • Project — the top-level unit; carries one central project number that all evidence, resourcing, and spend attach to.
  • Stage — a phase in the delivery lifecycle.
  • Gate — the checkpoint at the end of a stage; closes only once every deliverable on its checklist has evidence, every compliance requirement for that stage is satisfied, and the sponsor signs off — unless a bypass or override has been exercised and recorded.
  • Deliverable — a required checklist item at a gate; needs an uploaded file as evidence.
  • Delivery Module — the configurable stage/gate/deliverable template for a given programme type.
  • Compliance Module — the regulatory requirements that run concurrently with a stage, specific to sector and jurisdiction.
  • Sector Variant — a rebranded, re-templated instance of the same engine for a different Hard FM vertical.
  • Approval Bucket — one of three Hard FM spend-approval categories a project's costs are classified under: Lifecycle Replacement, Small Works, or Variation. A single project can draw from more than one.

Delivery and compliance as two distinct, concurrently-running module types is explicit in the source note — it's the one piece of architecture already specified up front.

06Functional requirements

Stage-Gate Engine

  • Configurable library of stage/gate templates per project type — not hardcoded to one programme shape.
  • Each gate has an editable deliverables checklist.
  • Gate status: Not started → In progress → Awaiting sign-off → Signed off (or Rejected — returns to In progress, with the Sponsor's written reason attached for the PM).
  • A gate cannot reach "Signed off" until every deliverable on its checklist has an uploaded file, or has been explicitly bypassed.
  • A gate cannot reach "Signed off" while a blocking compliance requirement is outstanding, or that block has been explicitly overridden.
  • Only the Project Sponsor can approve a gate as ready and pass it under normal conditions. No other role can.
  • Only the Project Manager can bypass an ordinary deliverable — skip its evidence requirement for that gate, with a mandatory written reason.
  • A deliverable can be flagged legally significant. A PM cannot bypass one of these — only the Compliance Officer or the SRO can, with a mandatory written reason.
  • Only a higher-tier authority above the Sponsor (the SRO) can override an outstanding compliance block and pass the gate anyway.
DecidedConfirmed: PM-bypass and Sponsor-approval are hard, role-locked actions — not configurable to other roles.
DecidedConfirmed: compliance blocks gate pass-through by default, for governance — the same hard-stop treatment as missing delivery evidence, not advisory. See the Compliance Module section below for the override mechanic that sits alongside it.
DecidedConfirmed: a PM cannot bypass a deliverable that carries legal weight. That escalates to Compliance Officer or SRO instead — bypass authority isn't uniformly PM-level, it depends on what the deliverable actually is. Every bypass, at any authority level, requires a written reason (also confirmed — see Deliverable & Evidence Management below).
DecidedConfirmed: rejecting a gate requires a written reason and returns it to the PM — the same mandatory-reason pattern as every bypass and override in the model.
DecidedConfirmed: the Health sector's standard Delivery Module template follows the RIBA Plan of Work 2020's eight stages — Strategic Definition, Preparation & Briefing, Concept Design, Spatial Coordination, Technical Design, Manufacturing & Construction, Handover, Use — as the default gate structure, rather than a bespoke shape per project. Validated against a real worked example (NHS Forth Valley UPS Replacement) with a full mandatory-deliverables list per stage and named gate approvers (Authorised Person, Authorising Engineer, Principal Designer) alongside the core PM/Sponsor/SRO roles.

Deliverable & Evidence Management

  • PM uploads one or more files against each deliverable — backed by Microsoft SharePoint via Graph API once a site is connected (app-only auth, no user sign-in); falls back to a local dev stub with zero configuration, so the demo works unchanged either way. See §08.
  • Every upload, bypass, and sign-off records who acted and when — a full audit trail.
  • Version history when a file is replaced before sign-off.
  • Every deliverable is either ordinary (PM can bypass, with reason) or flagged legally significant (only Compliance Officer or SRO can bypass, with reason) — set when the deliverable template is authored.
DecidedConfirmed: audit trail and version control are required, not optional — this is now a firm requirement rather than an inferred one. Bypass reason is mandatory at every authority level.

Compliance Module — architecture options

This section assesses two ways to build this: a config-driven rules engine versus pluggable, code-level workflow modules per sector/regulation.

Config-driven rules engine
  • A compliance requirement is data: "at Stage X, in Sector Y, this evidence/attestation is required, blocks gate: yes/no."
  • Compliance Officer maintains rule sets through the same checklist/evidence primitives already built for the Stage-Gate Engine — one engine serves both.
  • New sector or new regulation = new config, no deploy.
  • Limited to what the rule schema can express — struggles with genuinely procedural logic (multi-step external verification, conditional branching unique to one regulator).
Pluggable workflow modules
  • Each regulation/sector is code behind a common interface — unlimited expressiveness, can call external systems, run arbitrary validation.
  • New sector or regulation needs a developer to build and ship a plugin — works against the "compliance officer self-serves" goal and slows the white-label sector-expansion model.
  • More surface area for bugs in logic that directly gates sign-off — higher-stakes code to maintain.
  • Inconsistent UX across plugins unless disciplined from the start.
Recommendation Start config-driven. What's described — required evidence per stage, scoped to a sector/jurisdiction, optionally gating — is structurally the same shape as the Delivery Module's checklist, so one generic checklist-plus-evidence-plus-gating engine can serve both delivery and compliance rather than building two subsystems. That directly serves the "compliance officer adds a rule set without a code change" goal and the multi-sector reuse model. Reserve a pluggable/code path only for the rare case that genuinely needs external logic (e.g. a live check against a regulator's API) — model that later as a special "external check" deliverable type inside the same config schema, rather than standing up a separate plugin architecture. This should be pressure-tested against one real compliance example (e.g. a specific fire-safety or HTM certification requirement) before it's locked in.
  • Compliance rules attach to a stage and run concurrently with delivery deliverables.
  • Rule sets are sector-specific — different content for Health vs. Defence vs. Justice.
  • Incomplete compliance status blocks gate sign-off by default, on par with missing delivery evidence.
  • A higher-tier authority above the Sponsor — provisionally a Senior Responsible Owner (SRO), the standard term for the accountable individual above a project sponsor in UK public-sector capital governance — can override that block and pass the gate anyway.
DecidedConfirmed: for true governance and control, compliance blocks gate pass-through by default. A super-level authority can still approve through it — an exception path for accepted risk, not a routine approval.
Note The escalation ladder turns out to be risk-based, not simply delivery-vs-compliance: a PM can bypass an ordinary deliverable without escalation — a procedural gap, lower stakes. Anything with legal weight — whether it's a delivery deliverable flagged legally significant, or a compliance requirement — escalates to Compliance Officer or SRO, because it means knowingly accepting regulatory or legal risk rather than skipping paperwork. Every level of override carries the same audit rigor — logged reason, timestamp, visible in the gate's history as an override, never rendered the same as an ordinary pass.
Decided Confirmed: the SRO role is assigned per project, like every other role — not held by a small fixed set of senior people across the tenant. This actually matches how SRO works in UK public-sector practice: each major programme has its own named SRO, not one central figure across an organisation, so the per-project model is the realistic one.
Decided Confirmed: an SRO override requires a written reason, captured at the point of override, and needs a single sign-off — not a multi-person approval chain. The written reason plus the SRO's identity and timestamp form the audit record for that gate.
Decided Confirmed: "Senior Responsible Owner (SRO)" stays as the title. The compliance-override mechanic is now fully specified end to end.

AI-Assisted Project Provisioning

The framing, 19 Aug 2026: the compliance corpus for a given sector/jurisdiction (e.g. the SHTM series, Building (Scotland) Act, HAI-SCRIBE for Scottish Health) is largely static — it doesn't change drastically once loaded — and the RIBA Plan of Work's eight stages are already a standard, sector-agnostic gate structure (see decided flag above). Given both of those as fixed background knowledge, a new project shouldn't need its Delivery Module hand-authored from scratch or picked from one hardcoded template — StageForge should be able to provision it from a short description of the project.

Full mechanism scoped separately in ProvisioningModel.html — summary below.

  • At project creation, the PM (or Compliance Officer) enters a free-text project description — e.g. "Replace 12 UPS units across 4 systems, weekend working, live hospital."
  • An LLM call matches that description against a library of pre-authored Templates (not freeform generation — see decided flag below) and proposes the closest Template plus a compliance-tag set; a Compliance Officer reviews and approves before the project goes live.
  • Building age is a determining input, not a footnote: which SHTM/HTM edition applied depends on when the hospital building was constructed, so a matched checklist should reference the standard in force at build time, not just the current edition.
  • The compliance corpus itself is managed, versioned reference data — maintained by the Compliance Officer once, reused across every project provisioned from it — not re-entered per project.
  • Provisioned output is a starting point, not a locked template: the PM/Compliance Officer retain the same edit rights over a provisioned checklist as over a hand-authored one before and during delivery.
Decided Confirmed: this depends on the Compliance Module existing as structured data first (Phase 2) — see roadmap, §09 — since matching is only as good as the corpus it's grounded in. The Compliance Module shipped 19 Aug 2026 (schema, live instantiation, gate-closure enforcement, UI), unblocking this.
Decided Revised 21 Aug 2026, once the Template library was built out to full coverage (17 Hard FM systems, up from the two it launched with): the bullet above describing an LLM call matching the description against the Template library is superseded. Real usage surfaced repeated mismatches — the LLM guessing the wrong discipline from an ambiguous brief, caught by the Compliance Officer on review every time. The read: which Hard FM system a project is about is a closed, known set the PM already knows outright, not something worth an LLM guess. /projects/new now carries an explicit "System / Template" dropdown instead — the PM picks the discipline directly, and the LLM's role narrows to proposing the compliance-tag set (occupied/live site, National Treatment Centre, etc.) for that already-known system. The Compliance Officer's review correspondingly narrows from "is this even the right template" to just "are these tags right." ProvisioningModel.html §01/§03/§07 still describe the original LLM-matches-template design as originally scoped and built — read alongside this note, not as still-current.
Decided Confirmed 19 Aug 2026: template-library selection, not freeform generation — an LLM matches and adapts against a human-authored template library rather than drafting checklist items from scratch, to bound the failure mode to "picked the wrong real template" rather than "hallucinated a regulatory requirement."
Decided Confirmed 19 Aug 2026: a provisioned project requires Compliance Officer review and approval before it goes live — resolves the human-review open question below. A provisioned project sits in draft, not instantiated, until reviewed.
Open question How much PM-facing guidance the platform should surface — a one-time generated brief the PM reads at project start, versus ongoing contextual guidance resurfaced at each gate — is undefined. ProvisioningModel.html assumes a one-time brief for a first version.
Decided Added 21 Aug 2026: Works Packages. A hospital runs 24/7, so a disruption window (an area decanted, a system isolated) is precious — the example: redesigning the main kitchen's drainage is reason enough to also upgrade its ventilation and relamp its lighting while it's cleared out. Rather than merging multiple disciplines' checklists into one project — which would blur the compliance rigor the Template model exists for, since each discipline needs its own SHTM reference, its own Authorised Person, its own sign-off chain — a project stays single-Template and fully correct, and can optionally link to a WorksPackage: a lightweight label grouping otherwise-independent projects sharing one disruption window. Set at creation (an existing-package dropdown or a new-package name field on /projects/new) or after the fact once a PM decides to fold in extra work. Shown as a small badge on the portfolio and each project's own dashboard, cross-linking to the sibling projects — no effect on instantiation, tags, or bypass/override authority.
Decided Refined same day, from the first reaction to the shipped feature: linking a project to a package was a fine label, but there was no fast way to actually build the package out, and no way to look at it as one thing. Two additions, no new concepts: a "+ Add a system" shortcut wherever a package badge appears, straight into /projects/new with that package already selected, so adding the second or third system is one click plus picking a Template, not re-finding the package in a dropdown; and a Works Package overview page — combined spend and combined outstanding deliverables/compliance summed across every project in the package, alongside each project's own row, so a PM or SRO can "assess the full project" as a whole disruption window, not just its individual pieces one dashboard at a time.
Decided Refined again same day: the actual, repeated point — "there are times when multiple systems from the 17 [templates] are required... there is no option to add those packages on the 'new project' screen right now" — was first answered with a second, separate collapsed form lower down the page for bulk-creating several systems at once, leaving the works-package box itself still a single-system tagging field. the reaction to that split — "the 'part of combined works package' window is useless as it is right now unless it adds additional system packages" — showed the two belonged together, not apart. Merged into one: the works-package box on /projects/new now carries the multi-system checklist directly, alongside the existing-package dropdown and new-package name field. The primary system is still picked the usual way above; any additional systems checked in the works-package box become sibling DRAFT projects created in the same submission, all linked to the same package (a package name, existing or new, becomes required the moment anything extra is checked, since that's what links them). Each sibling still needs its own Compliance Officer review before going live, same as a solo project.

Roles & Permissions

  • Roles (PM, Sponsor, SRO, FM Contractor, Client Authority, Compliance Officer, Resource/Portfolio Manager, Finance) are global definitions; who holds each role is assigned per project/programme.
  • Bypassing an ordinary deliverable is locked to Project Manager only.
  • Bypassing a legally significant deliverable is locked to Compliance Officer or SRO only — not the PM.
  • Gate approval under normal conditions is locked to Project Sponsor only.
  • Overriding a blocked compliance requirement is locked to the SRO role only — a tier above the Sponsor, not an alternate route to the same permission.
  • Every bypass and every override requires a written reason and a single sign-off — no multi-person approval chain at any level.
DecidedConfirmed: role assignment happens at project/programme configuration, and permissions are required from the outset, not a later hardening pass.
Decided Revised 20–21 Aug 2026, once the Template library exposed real, named Trust appointments the global role list above didn't cover: Fire Officer, Authorised Person (Electrical), Authorised Person (Water), Authorised Person (Ventilation), Authorised Person (Medical Gases), Clinical Safety Officer, Information Governance Officer, and Principal Designer are now part of the role roster — added only where a genuinely distinct, real appointment exists, never invented for an external statutory regime that doesn't correspond to one (e.g. no "Gas AP" for fuel gas, no "Lifts AP" for LOLER — those stay SRO/Compliance-Officer checkpoints). The bypass model above was also corrected from a simple PM → Compliance Officer → SRO ladder to a mixed model: SRO remains an apex that can act at any lower tier, but every other named authority — Fire Officer, each Authorised Person, Clinical Safety Officer, Information Governance Officer — is an exact-match requirement even Compliance Officer doesn't inherit into. A fire-safety item needs the Fire Officer specifically; an isolation/permit item needs the relevant discipline AP specifically. This closed two real bugs: fire-safety items had been escalating to SRO, who has no legal standing over fire compliance, and Compliance Officer bypass authority had been silently reaching down into ordinary PM-tier deliverables it was never tagged for.
Decided Added 23 Aug 2026: a platform-admin-only Team page (/team) is the in-app replacement for hand-editing seed data to add a person, correct their name/email, or put them on a project — previously the only way to do any of this. Editing a person's name/email in place is explicitly scoped as a typo/contact-detail fix, not a "this person left" workflow — renaming rewrites how that person's past actions display too (every audit entry, evidence upload, and sign-off joins live to their name). A genuine handover — someone leaves, someone else takes the role — is a new person plus reassigning their project role, so audit history stays correctly attributed to whoever actually acted. Configurable multi-party approval (more than one role independently required to clear a compliance item, e.g. Compliance Officer and a named NHS-side approver) was raised the same day as a related, larger follow-on — not yet designed.

Financial View

  • Shows actual spend — all invoices — rolled up against the central project number.
  • Spend is classified into the five Approval Buckets Hard FM uses: Lifecycle Replacement, Small Works, Variation, Serco Investment, and Capital Project. Each bucket carries its own approval route.
  • A single large project can draw from more than one bucket at once — the Financial View must show total spend across all buckets combined for that project, as well as broken out per bucket, so a Sponsor or SRO sees full committed spend rather than one approval stream's slice of it.
  • Longer-term ambition to connect that project number back to SAP or D365.
DecidedConfirmed 19 Aug 2026: the original three approval buckets are Lifecycle Replacement, Small Works, and Variation; large projects often draw from more than one, and spend must roll up across all of them a project uses, not just per bucket. Extended 11 Sep 2026 with two more, from real user feedback: Serco Investment (Serco's own capital committed to a project, not client-funded) and Capital Project (a distinct capital works programme, as opposed to routine lifecycle replacement of an existing asset) — same rollup rule applies across all five.
DecidedConfirmed: SAP/D365 integration is not near-term. Financial view should ship with manual/invoice-level actuals only; ERP reconciliation stays a later-phase ambition.
Open question Approval thresholds and workflow differences between the five buckets — value ranges, whether each bucket has its own sign-off role beyond Sponsor/SRO, whether bucket choice is set per-deliverable or per-project — aren't specified yet. Sector / Brand Variants & Tenancy

Same underlying engine, different template libraries and branding per sector. Health is the reference implementation.

Decided Confirmed: multi-tenancy is the right model — many projects and different company/programme departments will use the same platform. This resolves the earlier open question and implies a shared backend with per-sector template libraries and branding, not separate codebases per sector.
Open question — worked example

What is the actual tenant boundary? A client company, or a department/programme within one? Take a realistic case:

BuildCare FM Ltd, an FM contractor, holds Hard FM contracts with six NHS Trusts, including St Aldwyn NHS Foundation Trust. Within St Aldwyn, two departments run separate capital programmes with different budget holders and different Sponsors — Estates & Facilities and Digital Transformation. BuildCare delivers to St Aldwyn through its North region team, organisationally separate from the South region team servicing a different Trust.

One live project: "Ward 12 HVAC Replacement," owned by St Aldwyn Estates, delivered by BuildCare North.

Who should see it:

  • BuildCare North's PM sees it — must not see BuildCare South's work for a different Trust, or St Aldwyn Digital Transformation's programme.
  • St Aldwyn Estates' Sponsor sees it — must not see BuildCare's contracts with other Trusts, or St Aldwyn Digital Transformation's programme.
  • St Aldwyn Digital Transformation and BuildCare South see none of it.
  1. Isolation has to nest at least two levels on both sides — Company → Department/Business-unit. "St Aldwyn" alone isn't tight enough (Estates and Digital Transformation must be walled off from each other); "BuildCare" alone isn't either (North and South must be).
  2. A single project is inherently shared across two different top-level companies at once — the FM Contractor and the Client Authority both need live access to the same gates, evidence, and sign-off. So "tenant" can't mean "the company that owns the data," the way it would in an ordinary single-company SaaS product — each project has its own small set of participating organisations, and visibility is really project-scoped, sitting underneath company/department as the unit of branding, admin, and billing.

The real decision isn't "which level is the tenant" — it's whether StageForge needs a two-tier tenancy model (Company → Department, for admin/branding/billing) with a separate project-level access-control layer underneath that grants specific companies/departments/people visibility into specific shared projects. That's a bigger data-model decision than picking a level, worth confirming before any schema work starts.

External Roster & Email-Based Approval (Project Managed System)

Origin: a 11 Sep 2026 product requirement ("Agent- Project Managager.docx") asking whether an AI Project Managed System could drive a StageForge Health project through every gate while replacing the native multi-login approval UI with email — only the PM holds a real StageForge login, every other dutyholder (Compliance Officer, an Authorised Person, a Water Group contact, etc.) is reached by email, with escalation on non-response, driven by a per-project team roster set up at project start and editable by the PM/admin thereafter.

Decided A gap analysis against the real codebase (schema, disciplineTeam.ts, lib/email.ts, the Electron/Health divergence) found the existing role/bypass-authority model is genuinely solid but strictly internal — every GateSignOff/DeliverableBypass requires an authenticated User, there was no external contact/roster model at all, email capability existed but was one-way and had exactly one caller (a passive weekly digest), and nothing routed a deliverable's bypassAuthority to an actual notification. Confirmed 11 Sep 2026: email approval is accepted as authentic evidence of a decision — but with a hard boundary, stated explicitly because it was the one point worth getting wrong: the Project Managed System is a relay and recorder, never a decision-maker. No timeout-based default-approve under any circumstance (escalation means notifying harder, never proceeding anyway), no inferring consent from ambiguous wording, and no path where the agent's own account substitutes its judgement for the registered person's. The write that ultimately lands in GateSignOff still has to point to a real User (schema requirement, unchanged) — proposed design: a new EmailApproval record (requester/role, verified sender email, thread reference, decision text, decided-at) linked from GateSignOff via a capturedVia: DIRECT | EMAIL_PROXY flag, so the account satisfying the foreign key is never mistaken, anywhere in the UI, for the actual decision-maker.
Decided Architecture: reuse rather than duplicate. StageForge stays the system of record (owns Project/Gate/Deliverable/bypassAuthority and the new roster/evidence tables) — but the actual email send/receive mechanics route through the sibling Group Discussion "AI Council" app's already-proven Graph API mailbox pipeline rather than building inbound email handling here from zero (StageForge's own lib/email.ts/Resend integration is outbound-only and has never received a reply). The orchestrator itself lives in that app too, alongside its other headless-agent tooling.
Decided ProjectContact shipped 11 Sep 2026 (Phase 1 of the build sequence below): name, email, an optional roleKey reusing the real Role.key vocabulary where it maps (falls back to free-text accountability where it doesn't, e.g. "Water Group contact" has no corresponding Role today), reversible-archive not hard-delete — same convention as User.archivedAt, since a contact may be the real named party a future EmailApproval references. Managed on the project dashboard, gated to the Project Manager role on that project (same authority boundary as setResourceAllocation/removeRoleAssignment). Not yet wired into any gate/notification/approval logic — deliberately just the roster itself so far.
Decided Phases 2 and 3 shipped 11 Sep 2026. EmailApproval (requester, roster contact, requestToken, status PENDING → SENT → DECIDED) is created via requestEmailApproval, gated to contacts whose roleKey === "SPONSOR" — the requirement that a matching roleKey is mandatory was confirmed explicitly, so an EmailApproval can only ever carry the same authority a real in-app Sponsor decision would. A small bearer-token API (/api/email-approvals/pending, /sent, /reply) is the only bridge to the sibling AI Council mailbox pipeline — chosen over direct DB access for isolation and independent scaling. The /reply route is the actual gatekeeper: it re-verifies sender against the roster's on-file address, refuses anything that isn't an explicit unambiguous APPROVED/REJECTED (never infers from wording), re-checks the contact still holds the Sponsor role, and enforces the exact same gate preconditions decide() (the in-app Sponsor action) does — before writing an EmailApproval decision and a GateSignOff tagged capturedVia: EMAIL_PROXY. The FK-satisfying service account (email-approval-system@stageforge.local) never signs in and is never shown in the UI as the decision-maker — GateDetail.tsx's decision history branches on capturedVia and always surfaces the real external contact's name/email instead. A permanent smoke test (scripts/smoke-test-email-approval-api.mts, 28 checks) calls the real route handlers directly against real local dev data and is fire-tested — deliberately broken and confirmed to fail, then reverted — to prove it actually catches drift, not just pass the happy path.
Decided Phase 4 shipped 11 Sep 2026. A pure, unit-testable policy function (computeOverdueAction) decides, per EmailApproval still stuck in SENT, whether it's due a reminder or an escalation: a fixed interval since the last nudge (sentAt or lastReminderAt, whichever is later) before the first reminder is due, a capped reminder count before escalation kicks in instead, and — critically — once escalated, that EmailApproval drops out of consideration permanently, so the system never nags automatically forever and never treats silence as anything but silence. An escalation notifies whoever originally requested the approval that their contact has gone unanswered and asks them to follow up directly or reassign it — it is a notification only, and never touches Gate.status or writes a GateSignOff under any circumstance. Verified with 11 new smoke-test checks (39 total), fire-tested (the escalation threshold comparison was deliberately broken, confirmed caught, then reverted), and a full live end-to-end run of the AI Council side against an isolated local dev server — a real reminder email sent and confirmed, then a real escalation email sent and confirmed.

07Non-functional requirements

None are stated in the source note. These are inferred from the domain; audit trail, versioning, and permissions are now confirmed requirements (see above) rather than inferred ones.

  • Data sensitivity — hospital capital-works data (and defence/justice variants later) implies access control, encryption at rest and in transit, and likely UK data residency.
  • Auditability — sign-off is a governance action; every gate decision, bypass, and upload needs an immutable audit trail of who, what evidence, and when. Decided
  • Extensibility — a compliance officer should be able to add a new sector's rule set through configuration, not a code change.
  • Multi-tenancy — data must be isolated per tenant (and possibly per department within a tenant — see open question above). Decided

08Integrations

  • SAP / D365 — for spend reconciliation. Confirmed not near-term; keep out of MVP and early phases entirely.
  • pmopassport.co.uk — a personally-owned domain, also used by the GasNet project. StageForge sits as a sibling subdomain, not a shared product. Whether it shares infrastructure or login with GasNet is still open.
  • Microsoft SharePoint — the organisation's approved safe storage for evidence files, since the NHS/Serco context rules out a bespoke file server. A Graph API client (app-only/client-credentials auth) is built and wired into the upload actions, gated behind whether a real site is connected — inactive by default, real evidence storage is a config step, not a code change. See §06 Deliverable & Evidence Management.
  • Group Discussion "AI Council" app — the sibling local app that owns every real headless nhs-scotland-* agent (document review and, now, document drafting), the Microsoft Graph mailbox pipeline (send/capture/remind/escalate), and the orchestrator that chains them. StageForge never calls an LLM or a mailbox directly — it stays the system of record and exposes a small bearer-token API (/api/email-approvals/*, /api/document-reviews/*, and the planned /api/document-generations/*) that the AI Council side polls. That polling direction only works because the AI Council app currently has no public URL of its own and the cloud web app does (via an existing Cloudflare Tunnel) — a real constraint once Electron enters the picture: Electron has no AI capability at all and, being a per-PM local install, no public URL either, so it cannot be polled the way the cloud app is. Reaching the AI Council from an Electron install means inverting the direction — Electron pushes a request out to a centralised, publicly-reachable AI Council endpoint and polls that endpoint for its own result — which itself first needs the AI Council app exposed via its own tunnel (the same Cloudflare Tunnel + password-auth pattern already running for this app, not a new mechanism). See §09 Phase 5.

09Proposed phased roadmap

Phase 0

Discovery & validation

Validate the stage-gate problem against 2–3 real Hard FM hospital programmes; pressure-test the config-driven compliance model against one real regulatory requirement; nail down the tenant boundary (company vs. department).

Phase 1

MVP — Hospital Hard FM, multi-tenant

Stage-Gate Engine + Deliverable/Evidence upload + role-locked sign-off (PM/Compliance Officer/SRO bypass depending on legal significance, Sponsor approve) + audit trail/versioning + role assignment per project. One template, Health only, multi-tenant from day one. No compliance module, no resource or finance views yet.

Phase 2

Compliance + Resourcing

Add the config-driven Compliance Module and the Resource/Capacity view, still Health-only. Once the compliance corpus exists as structured data, layer AI-assisted project provisioning on top of it — generating a new project's gate/deliverable/authority checklist and PM guidance from a free-text project description, grounded in that corpus rather than free-generated.

Phase 3

Finance view + second sector

Financial view (manual/invoice actuals, no ERP yet), spend classified into the five Hard FM approval buckets — Lifecycle Replacement, Small Works, Variation, Serco Investment, Capital Project — with combined spend rolled up across all five where a project draws from more than one; begin templating a second sector to prove the white-label model actually holds. SAP/D365 integration deferred beyond this.

Phase 4

Project Managed System — external roster & email-based approval

Six sub-phases, each independently verified against real data before the next starts, per the 11 Sep 2026 gap analysis above: (1) ProjectContact roster model — shipped; (2) notification routing — StageForge creates a pending EmailApproval request, the AI Council mailbox pipeline sends it with a reply-tracking reference — shipped; (3) verified inbound approval capture — matches a reply to its request, verifies the sender against the roster, requires an explicit unambiguous decision, writes EmailApproval + a GateSignOff tagged capturedVia: EMAIL_PROXY — shipped, smoke/fire-tested, and verified end-to-end against a real inbound reply on both sides (StageForge's route and the AI Council mailbox reply-scanner); (4) escalation/reminder scheduling — shipped and verified end-to-end: GET /api/email-approvals/overdue computes, per EmailApproval, whether it's due a reminder to the same contact or an escalation to whoever requested it (graduated policy — a fixed reminder interval, a capped reminder count, then escalate once and stop chasing automatically), and the AI Council side sends the actual reminder/escalation email and confirms back via POST .../reminded or .../escalated; escalating only ever notifies a human harder, it never advances the gate itself; (5) the StageForge↔AI Council document bridge — shipped and verified end-to-end: the PM explicitly requests a review of one real, already-submitted evidence file, naming which of the five reviewable nhs-scotland-* agents applies (no automatic document-type detection); GET /api/document-reviews/pending exposes the request to the AI Council mailbox pipeline, which downloads the real evidence via GET .../evidence, runs the named agent headlessly against it, and uploads the resulting report back via POST .../complete as a new EvidenceFile tagged kind: AI_REVIEW on the same Deliverable — never SUBMITTED, so it can never supersede or be mistaken for the real evidence it reviewed; a genuine failure (bad input, agent error) is recorded via .../failed rather than silently vanishing. Verified with a real byte-level round trip (upload → download → agent review → report upload) against real local evidence storage, and both the happy path and a deliberately provoked failure path proven live end-to-end; (6) the orchestrator — shipped and verified end-to-end, built last and deliberately the narrowest-scope version of "orchestrator" that could reasonably carry the name: a single unattended entry point (run-orchestrator.mjs) that runs the four already-proven AI Council pipelines in sequence — send pending requests, capture replies, chase overdue ones, run pending document reviews. It initiates nothing a PM hasn't already asked for in the StageForge UI; every step it runs only ever acts on a request/reply/review that already exists. A failure in one step is logged and does not block the others — fire-tested by deliberately breaking one step and confirming the remaining three still ran and the failure was correctly reported, not silently swallowed or allowed to halt the run.

Phase 5

AI-integrated evolution — document-generation bridge, generalised email accountability, Electron reach

Confirmed direction 12 Sep 2026, scoped deliberately narrow for the first build against this same live, single-database system — same "prove each piece against real data before combining" discipline as Phases 1–6. Five sub-items, ordered by what's safe to build immediately versus what needs its own proven increment first. (1)–(3) all shipped and verified end-to-end the same day; (4)–(5) remain deliberately deferred.

(1) Expose the AI Council app via its own public endpoint — a Cloudflare Tunnel + password auth, the exact pattern already running for this StageForge deployment, not a new mechanism. Useful in its own right (remote access to the AI Council app from anywhere) independent of everything else here, and the real prerequisite for (5) below. Shipped — new named tunnel (ai-council), DNS routed, Windows service installed and running, real HTTP 200 confirmed through the public URL. Password auth (Cloudflare Access) intentionally left for a separate follow-up — no dashboard/API access to configure it from here.

(2) Document-generation bridge — purely additive to the schema (new EvidenceFileKind.AI_DRAFT, new DocumentGenerationRequest/DocumentGenerationSource models, no existing table changes shape), so safe against live data. Adds nhs-scotland-sow-generator, nhs-scotland-business-case-generator, and nhs-scotland-ccn-preparation as drafting integrations alongside Phase 5's five review-only agents, with a project-wide source-file picker (a generator's inputs can come from a different Gate/Deliverable than its target — the review bridge never needed this, since it only ever reviews the one file it's pointed at). Shipped — schema migrated live, 5 new API routes + server action + UI form all built and type-checked, a real request/poll/download/fail cycle proven end-to-end against a real demo-project deliverable (the happy path — real file bytes in, real draft out — is blocked on this deployment having no evidence storage configured at all yet, SharePoint or local; a pre-existing gap, not something this bridge introduced).

(3) Generalised, multi-contact, tiered-escalation email approval — the highest-value and highest-risk piece, so scoped narrowest: generalise requestEmailApproval/the /reply route beyond the hardcoded SPONSOR check to any role with a configured routing rule, add multi-contact simultaneous routing and a per-role escalation tree (peer-only, hierarchy-escalation, or both — see §06), and make a timed-out tier actually advance and re-notify with full content rather than only nagging the requester as today's Phase 4 does. Deliberately kept to the Gate-level decision path that already works end-to-end for this pass — DeliverableBypass/ComplianceOverride/ComplianceCoSignOff have no email-proxy today at all and are real future work, not a small extension. Shipped — see the Shipped flag under §06 for the full build and the three real issues (two bugs, one defensive guard) its own fire-testing caught and fixed before being trusted.

(4) Explicitly deferred, not part of this pass: migrating any real seeded person (e.g. the SRO or Fire Officer personas) from a real login to a contact-only record. prisma/seed.ts is not safely re-runnable — it uses db.user.create, not upsert, and its comments narrate real production history (a live persona reassignment, a real reported sign-in bug) against the one Postgres database this exact dev checkout and the live pm2-served stageforge.pmopassport.co.uk both read. Any such migration is a separate, explicitly-triggered step once (3) is proven, reusing the existing User.archivedAt soft-delete (already blocks sign-in, already preserves every FK a real historical actor needs) rather than a schema redesign.

(5) Electron push integration — once (1) exists, wire Electron's local instance to push document-generation and email-approval requests out to the AI Council's public endpoint and poll it for results, inverting today's cloud-web-app pattern (AI Council polls StageForge) since Electron has neither AI capability nor a public URL of its own to be polled at. Not yet scoped in detail — needs its own investigation of the Electron codebase's networking before a concrete build plan.

Longer-term, not yet a committed phase: an AI-driven orchestration layer that goes beyond Phase 6's "run the already-authorised mechanical steps" and actively surfaces what's ready, chases what's overdue, and drives approvals end-to-end — human PM, AI Council, and this orchestration layer working as one system, with the PM as the ultimate control point rather than the one operating every step by hand.
