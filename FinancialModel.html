StageForge — Financial Model

Technical scoping — Draft v0.1
Owner: Kevin Fotheringham · 19 Aug 2026
Source: PRD.html §06 "Financial View" + DataModel.html §04 (SpendRecord sketch) + Kevin's decisions, 19 Aug 2026
Scoping — not built

Working out the last unbuilt piece from the PRD's Phase 2–3 roadmap — actual spend, checked and approved at each gate, classified into the three Hard FM approval buckets, with a real approval step rather than passive logging.

01Starting boundary

Already decided before this document, from the PRD:

  • Manual/invoice-level actuals only. PRD.html §06 decided flag: SAP/D365 integration is not near-term — ship with manual entry, ERP reconciliation stays a later-phase ambition.
  • Three approval buckets. Lifecycle Replacement, Small Works, Variation — a single large project can draw from more than one at once, and the view must show total spend across all buckets combined and broken out per bucket.
  • Gate-level, not just project-level. Revised by Kevin, 19 Aug 2026, superseding this document's original v0.1 draft (which read spend as project-level only): spend must be checked and approved at each gate. A SpendRecord attaches to a Gate — the same shape as Deliverable and ComplianceRequirement — and an unapproved blocking record holds up that gate's Sponsor submission exactly the way an unevidenced deliverable does. A project's total is still the sum across its gates, reached via Gate → Stage → Project, not a direct FK.
  • The Finance role already exists, seeded, unused (roleDefs in seed.ts) — PRD.html §04: "Reconciles invoice-level actual spend against the central project number."
Decided Confirmed by Kevin, 19 Aug 2026: spend needs a real approval step, not passive recording — the PRD's own unresolved open question ("approval thresholds and workflow differences between the three buckets... whether each bucket has its own sign-off role") is resolved in the direction of building governance here, mirroring the gate-closure pattern used everywhere else in the app rather than treating Financial View as a simple log.

02What's already built and reusable

This is the fourth time this exact shape has been needed — GateSignOff, ProvisioningReview, and ComplianceOverride all follow the same pattern: a record entity, a decision-history entity shaped like it (not flat columns on the record — read the latest row instead of duplicating state), a mandatory reason on anything other than approval, and an AuditLogEntry alongside every action. Spend approval is the same pattern a fourth time, not a new one.

Net-new work Two entities (SpendRecord, SpendApproval), an approval-authority rule (§04 — the genuinely undecided part), three actions, and a view — gate-level plus a portfolio-wide Finance route, mirroring /resources.

03Data model

Entity / fieldPurpose
ApprovalBucketNew enum: LIFECYCLE_REPLACEMENT | SMALL_WORKS | VARIATION.
SpendRecordid, gateId, bucket, amount (Decimal), description, invoiceReference (String?), blocksGate (Boolean, default true), recordedById, status (PENDING | APPROVED), createdAt. One row per invoice/actual, not per gate — a gate's total is the sum of its records. blocksGate mirrors Deliverable/ComplianceRequirement.
SpendApprovalid, spendRecordId, decision (APPROVED | REJECTED), approvedById, reason (String?), createdAt. Shaped exactly like GateSignOff/ProvisioningReview.
Decided No REJECTED terminal status on SpendRecord — same reasoning as every other status enum in this app (GateStatus, ProjectStatus): a rejection is a SpendApproval(REJECTED, reason) row, and the record simply stays PENDING, editable, visible to Finance with the reason attached, not a dead end.
Decided amount is Decimal, not Int/Float — money needs exact arithmetic; Postgres numeric via Prisma's Decimal type is the correct fit, unlike ResourceAllocation.allocationPercent which is a legitimate whole-number Int. GBP only, no multi-currency — every example in every source document is UK-only.

04Approval authority — the genuinely undecided part

The PRD flagged, unresolved, whether buckets have different sign-off roles or value thresholds. Real Hard FM practice likely does vary this by bucket and amount, but no specific rule survives in any source document — inventing numbers here would be guessing at someone else's finance policy.

Decided (revised 22 Aug 2026) Real practice is the other way round from the v1 recommendation below: the PM records spend (the delivery owner logging what's been spent, same as deliverable/compliance evidence — canUploadEvidence/canUploadComplianceEvidence's "PM does the day-to-day work" pattern), and Finance approves it (the domain owner checking the PM's entry), with SRO able to do either (the existing tiered-authority pattern). Sponsor is not part of the spend loop at all — its role in this app is the separate gate-level sign-off decision (canDecideGate), which still can't happen until spend clears here too (isGateReadyForSponsor) — two distinct checks, not one role rubber-stamping the other.
Superseded recommendation (19 Aug 2026) Ship v1 with a single, flat rule reusing authority the app already has: Sponsor approves (same actor who approves gates — PRD.html §06 already casts the Sponsor as the accountable approver for a project), SRO can also approve (the existing tiered-authority pattern — whoever can do more can do less), identical across all three buckets, and no amount-based threshold (every spend record needs sign-off regardless of size). This is deliberately the simplest rule that satisfies "add an approval step" without guessing at bucket- or value-specific policy that isn't written down anywhere yet.
Decided Since spend now attaches to a Gate (§01), it folds into the same closure check as everything else: isGateReadyForSponsor(deliverables, complianceRequirements, spendRecords) gained a third parameter — delivery, compliance, and spend are three concurrently-running checks, all three must clear, none substitutes for another. A gate with zero spend recorded against it is vacuously ready on the spend dimension, same as zero deliverables or zero compliance requirements.
Open question Whether Lifecycle Replacement, Small Works, and Variation should actually require different approval tiers or value thresholds in practice — the exact question the PRD already left open, still open. If Kevin's real process differs (e.g. Small Works auto-approves under some value, Lifecycle Replacement always needs SRO), that's a straightforward extension of the same rule shape once the actual numbers exist.

05Actions

ActionWhoEffect
recordSpendPM or SRO (gate-scoped)Creates a SpendRecord against a Gate — bucket, amount, description, invoice reference — status PENDING. Flips a NOT_STARTED gate to IN_PROGRESS, same as the first evidence upload on that gate.
approveSpendFinance or SRO (§04)Records SpendApproval(APPROVED), flips SpendRecord.status to APPROVED.
rejectSpendFinance or SRO, reason requiredRecords SpendApproval(REJECTED, reason). Record stays PENDING — same "return to editable state with a reason" pattern as gate rejection and provisioning revision.

Every action also writes the usual AuditLogEntry — spend.recorded, spend.approved, spend.rejected — same append-only trail as everything else.

Deferred reviseSpend (editing a PENDING record's amount/bucket/description before approval) was in the original v1 sketch but isn't built — see §08. Today, a mistaken entry stays visible and blocking until Finance and the approver sort it out via reject-then-record-again; no data is silently lost either way.

07Explicitly out of scope here

  • ERP integration (SAP/D365) — PRD.html §06/§08, confirmed not near-term.
  • Amount-based approval thresholds (§04) — flat rule for v1, revisit once real threshold numbers exist.
  • Multi-currency.
  • Budget-vs-actual — this tracks actuals only, no budget/forecast figure to compare against. Would need its own scoping if wanted.
  • reviseSpend — see §05, deferred not out-of-scope.

08Open questions

  1. Bucket- or value-specific approval authority (§04) — the PRD's own unresolved question, still unresolved, shipped with a deliberately simple default in the meantime.
  2. Whether Finance should be able to delete a PENDING record they made in error, or whether every record — even a mistaken one — stays in the audit trail forever with a note. Leaning toward the latter (matches the "no undo, ever" philosophy already established for bypasses and overrides), not decided.
  3. A project-level (rather than per-gate) rolled-up spend total and the portfolio-wide /finance route are both still unbuilt — the gate-level record/approve loop is the piece that actually needed to exist for spend to be checked and approved at each gate; the read-only roll-up views are a natural next increment.
